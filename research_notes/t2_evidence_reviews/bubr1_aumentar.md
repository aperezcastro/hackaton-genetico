# Drugs/supplements reported to increase BUBR1 (BUB1B) protein or spindle checkpoint function

## Q1. Is there direct evidence that NAD+ precursors / SIRT2 raise BUBR1 levels, and with what functional benefit?

### Takeaway
The only direct drug-to-BubR1 evidence is the Sinclair/van Deursen line: NAD+ decline -> less SIRT2 activity -> CBP-acetylated BubR1 K668 -> degradation; NMN (i.p., 500 mg/kg/day x 7 d) raised BubR1 in mouse testes, and SIRT2 overexpression extended lifespan in BubR1-hypomorphic mice. Evidence is mouse/oocyte only, with no human or MVA-patient data found.

### Cited Findings
- BubR1 loss with age is attributed to NAD+ decline and reduced SIRT2 ability to keep K668 deacetylated (CBP counteracts); SIRT2 overexpression or NMN increases BubR1 abundance in vivo (North et al., EMBO J 2014, PMID 24825348, DOI 10.15252/embj.201386907) — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24825348/); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4194088)
- NMN protocol: 500 mg/kg/day i.p. in PBS for 7 days; BubR1 increased in testes of young and aged mice; aged heart BubR1 could not be measured reliably — [PMC4194088](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4194088)
- K668Q (acetyl-mimetic) accelerates degradation; K668R slows it — [PMC4194088](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4194088)
- SIRT2 overexpression in BubR1 H/H (hypomorph) mice: median lifespan +58% overall (reported +123% in males, no significant change in females), maximal lifespan +21% — [PMC4194088](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4194088); [search summary of PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24825348/)
- Limitation acknowledged: SIRT2 overexpression did not restore BubR1 to wild-type levels, so lordokyphosis and cataracts were not reversed — [PMC4194088](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4194088)
- Oocytes: SIRT2 is reduced in aged mouse oocytes; acetylation of BubR1 K243 matters in meiosis; BubR1-K243R overexpression lowers spindle/chromosome anomalies and aneuploid eggs (Zhang et al., Aging Cell, 2018) — [PMC5770883](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5770883/) (PMID not verified in this session)
- Cell Reports 2020 (30(6):1670-1681, Bertoldo et al.): NAD+ declines with age in oocytes; NMN rejuvenates oocyte quality in aged mice, reproduced by SIRT2 overexpression — [PMC7063679](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7063679/) (PMID not verified)
- Other lysine sites/deacetylases exist: SIRT2 deacetylation of BubR1 K250 affects degradation at prometaphase/anaphase; HDAC2/3 deacetylation of BubR1 initiates SAC silencing (cited in search results, primary papers not opened) — see search hits e.g. [PMC4194088](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4194088)
- Oocyte NMN literature is heterogeneous (doses 200-500 mg/kg/day i.p.; mostly mice/pigs; authors call for standardised protocols and trials); that review does not mention BubR1 or SIRT2 — [Systematic review, J Assist Reprod Genet 2025, PMID 41160202, DOI 10.1007/s10815-025-03720-1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12831783/)

### Inferences
- NMN/NR (and niacin as cheaper NAD+ precursor, not directly tested for BubR1) is a mechanistically plausible but only mouse-supported lever; benefit depends on a residual BubR1 allele being stabilised by deacetylation. If the patient's second allele is a missense with normal stability but reduced function, stabilisation may add little; if hypomorphic via instability, more plausible.
- A truncating allele (NMD) is unaffected by K668 stabilisation; only the remaining allele's protein can be increased.
- The i.p. 500 mg/kg dose is far above human oral dosing; human relevance of the BubR1 effect is unproven.

### Gaps
- No human, MVA-patient or patient-fibroblast study of NMN/NR/niacin on BubR1 found.
- Could not open PubMed abstract (cookie wall); PMIDs for the 2018 Aging Cell and 2020 Cell Reports papers not verified.
- No test of NR (vs NMN) on BubR1 found; no 2020-2026 replication of the K668 mechanism found in my searches.

## Q2. Other approaches: transcriptional (FOXM1), overexpression, senolytics, proteostasis

### Takeaway
Genetic BubR1 overexpression is strongly protective in mice, but no drug has been shown to reproduce it. FOXM1 drives BUB1B transcription but is an oncogenic axis (targeted for inhibition), and no proteostasis drug specifically raising BubR1 was found.

### Cited Findings
- Transgenic sustained high BubR1 expression reduced aneuploidy and tumours (33% vs 100% in a Ras-driven context in the press summary), improved muscle, kidney, cardiac and retinal ageing, and extended healthy lifespan (Baker et al., Nat Cell Biol 2013, PMID 23242215) — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23242215/); [Mayo summary](https://newsnetwork.mayoclinic.org/discussion/mayo-clinic-study-unmasks-regulator-of-healthy-life-span)
- Oocytes: BubR1 overexpression protects aged oocytes from aneuploidy and extends reproductive lifespan (GEO GSE57482 entry) — [OmicsDI](https://www.omicsdi.org/dataset/geo/GSE57482)
- FOXM1 binds and activates BUB1 (and BUB1B) promoters; the FOXM1/BUB1B axis is pro-tumourigenic (glioblastoma, HCC, leukaemia); FOXM1 inhibitors (FDI-6, RCM-1, thiostrepton) are being studied to reduce it, i.e. the pharmacological direction is down, not up — [Int J Biol Sci 2026;22(6):2986, DOI 10.7150/ijbs.125454](https://ijbs.com/v22p2986.htm); [FOXM1/BUB1B glioblastoma](https://scholar.xjtu.edu.cn/en/publications/the-foxm1bub1b-signaling-pathway-is-essential-for-the-tumorigenic/)
- Senolytics (not raising BubR1 but treating its consequences): clearing p16Ink4a+ senescent cells in BubR1 progeroid mice delayed sarcopenia, cataracts and fat loss (Baker et al., Nature 2011, PMID 22048312) — [Mayo](https://mayoclinic.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/clearance-of-p16-ink4a-positive-senescent-cells-delays-ageing-ass); [PMC3468323](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3468323). Used a genetic INK-ATTAC transgene, not an approved drug.
- BubR1 is degraded via APC/C-ubiquitin-proteasome, sumoylation and caspase cleavage; USP7 interacts with Bub3 (inhibition causes mitotic abnormalities). Search found no p38/HSP90/proteasome-inhibitor strategy that safely raises BubR1 — [PMC3258114](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3258114/figure/F6); [Oncotarget 1989](https://oncotarget.com/article/1989/text/)
- MVA/PCS patient cells: ectopic BUBR1 expression or transfer of chromosome 15 restored BubR1 levels and checkpoint function (proof of cause, not a drug) — [PMC2887387](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2887387); [Frontiers Cell Dev Biol 2023, DOI 10.3389/fcell.2023.1282182](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cell-and-developmental-biology/articles/10.3389/fcell.2023.1282182/pdf)

### Inferences
- Gene-level rescue (AAV/mRNA, read-through) is the only demonstrated way to raise BubR1 in patient cells; drugs remain indirect (NAD+, senolytics).
- Beware: any FOXM1 activation is pro-oncogenic, a poor idea in a child with a rhabdomyosarcoma history.

### Gaps
- No 2020-2026 preprint/paper found showing a small molecule that raises BubR1 or SAC strength in cells or mice (limited searches; not exhaustive, no bioRxiv search done).
- Resveratrol/rapamycin/metformin effects on BubR1 specifically: not found.

## Q3. Contrary evidence and safety

### Takeaway
Human NMN trials confirm raised blood NAD+ but modest/no effect on ageing endpoints, and low SAC function can increase sensitivity to antimitotics, while restoring a strong SAC may alter chemotherapy responses in either direction.

### Cited Findings
- Human RCT (80 adults, 60 days, 300/600/900 mg NMN): dose-dependent rise in blood NAD+, safe; efficacy on ageing markers modest; a 12-week 125 mg x2/day trial (n=36) showed no significant difference in arterial stiffness — [aggregate summaries: DOAJ Sci Rep 2023](https://doaj.org/article/2f8d89e369a942b6885b4956c5b26a28), [PMC10721522](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10721522/) (secondary summaries; primary papers not read)
- No evidence these trials measured BubR1 — absence in cited sources.
- SAC function modulates antimitotic drug response: suppressing Mad2/BubR1 produced paclitaxel resistance; conversely, higher BubR1 expression correlated with paclitaxel sensitivity in some lines; Mad2 overexpression increased vincristine sensitivity (older studies; Nature 2004 hit https://www.nature.com/articles/1206069) — [Fujita record](https://pure.fujita-hu.ac.jp/en/publications/dependence-of-paclitaxel-sensitivity-on-a-functional-spindle-asse/); [MCT 2020 PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10115222/1/1535-7163.MCT-20-0363.full.pdf)
- NAD+ boosting and cancer: not examined in this session (gap); BubR1 elevated in many tumours as a poor-prognosis marker — see FOXM1 hits above.

### Inferences
- Because the child's tumour risk is high and NMN effects on BubR1 are untested clinically, NAD+ precursors should be framed as hypothesis-generating, with oncologic and vincristine-interaction caveats.

### Gaps
- Dedicated search for NAD+ precursors and tumour growth/rhabdomyosarcoma not done.
- Primary human NMN trial citations lack PMIDs (only aggregator URLs retrieved).
