# NAD⁺ precursors and tumour risk — verified by the coordinator (2 Oct 2026)

**Why this matters:** H1 (raise BUBR1 via the NAD⁺/SIRT2 axis) is our top-ranked candidate line, and nicotinamide
its lead drug. The patient has had **rhabdomyosarcoma (RMS)** and a cancer-predisposition syndrome. Before
recommending a NAD⁺ precursor we must know whether raising NAD⁺ could feed a tumour.

All PMIDs below were retrieved and their abstracts read directly from PubMed/Europe PMC.

## Evidence AGAINST raising NAD⁺ in this patient

| PMID | Source | Finding | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| **37616468** | McKay-Corkum GB et al., *Clin Cancer Res* 2023;29(21):4479–91 (NCI) | **RMS is dependent on NAMPT**, the rate-limiting enzyme of the NAD⁺ salvage pathway. The clinical NAMPT inhibitor OT-82 depleted NAD⁺ and inhibited growth at ≤1 nmol/L in **all 10** molecularly diverse RMS cell lines, caused ATP depletion and irreversible necrosis, and produced **complete tumour regression** in orthotopic in vivo models. Authors call for clinical study of NAMPT inhibitors in RMS. | **High and directly on-target**: same tumour type as the patient; the therapeutic direction for RMS is the *opposite* of H1 |
| 36371959 | Maric T et al., *Biosens Bioelectron* 2023;220:114826 | NR supplementation **significantly increased cancer prevalence and brain metastases** in a triple-negative breast cancer animal model. Authors conclude NR use needs to be personalised in certain patient populations. | Moderate: different tumour, animal model, but same intervention class |
| 41724424 | Nakazzi F et al., *Cancer Lett* 2026;645:218334 | Vitamin B3 derivatives support pancreatic cancer cell survival and **chemotherapy resistance**. | Moderate: different tumour; raises a chemoresistance concern |

**Biochemical note.** Nicotinamide (NAM) is the direct **substrate of NAMPT** (NAM + PRPP → NMN). Giving NAM feeds
exactly the enzymatic step that RMS depends on. Nicotinamide riboside (NR) bypasses NAMPT (NRK1/2: NR → NMN), but
still raises the NAD⁺ pool that the tumour needs, so bypassing NAMPT does not remove the concern.

## Evidence IN FAVOUR of nicotinamide safety

| PMID | Source | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **26488693** | Chen AC et al., *N Engl J Med* 2015;373:1618–26 (ONTRAC) | Phase 3 RCT, 386 adults with ≥2 prior non-melanoma skin cancers. Nicotinamide 500 mg twice daily for 12 months **reduced new non-melanoma skin cancers by 23 %** (95 % CI 4–38, p = 0.02) and actinic keratoses, with no noteworthy difference in adverse events. Benefit did not persist after stopping. |
| 11126400 | Knip M et al., *Diabetologia* 2000 | Review of high-dose nicotinamide safety. |
| 15043959 | Gale EA et al., *Lancet* 2004 (ENDIT) | Large RCT of nicotinamide including children; safety record. |

**Important caveat on ONTRAC:** keratinocyte carcinoma is driven by UV-induced DNA damage, and nicotinamide's benefit
there is attributed to enhanced DNA repair and prevention of UV-induced ATP depletion. That biology does **not**
transfer to a NAMPT-dependent sarcoma. ONTRAC supports *tolerability*, not *oncological safety in RMS*.

## Interpretation for the report

This is a **genuine, unresolved conflict at the centre of our top candidate line**, and it must be stated plainly:

1. The mechanism we want (more NAD⁺ → SIRT2 keeps BUBR1 deacetylated → more BUBR1 → fewer missegregations → in
   principle *less* long-term cancer risk) runs in the opposite direction to the metabolic dependency of the tumour
   type this child has already had.
2. The two effects operate on different timescales: BUBR1 stabilisation is a chronic, preventive mechanism; NAMPT
   dependency is an acute requirement of an **existing** tumour mass.
3. Practical consequence — H1 should be conditioned, not deleted:
   - **Do not** propose a NAD⁺ precursor while there is active or recently treated RMS.
   - Any n-of-1 should require documented remission plus intensified tumour surveillance (AACR 2024 protocol,
     PMID 39264246) as a stopping rule.
   - Step 1 of the validation plan should test whether nicotinamide/NR actually raises BUBR1 **in this patient's
     cells**; if it does not, the risk is not worth taking at all.
   - Flag the mirror-image risk: if this child ever needs RMS therapy, a NAMPT inhibitor would be a rational agent,
     and a NAD⁺ supplement would be expected to antagonise it. This belongs on the safety card.
4. Scoring consequence: add a contrary-evidence entry to H1 (same-disease, in vivo tumour data) and a patient-specific
   safety flag. Expect nicotinamide to drop in the ranking.
